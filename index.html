How things work

House manual

Heating and cooling +

The Nest thermostat runs on an automatic schedule, so you usually won't need to touch it. If a room feels off, turn the ring to nudge the temperature and it will pick the schedule back up on its own.

The house has two levels and warm air rises, so the lower level can run cool. There are space heaters in both downstairs bedroom closets. Plug them only into the marked outlets in those bedrooms. Those outlets are on their own circuit, so the heaters won't trip a breaker.

Like most Tahoe homes, there's no air conditioning, and it's rarely needed up here. Even in summer the nights cool off, so open the windows in the evening to let the cool air in, then close them and the shades in the morning to keep the heat out.

TVs & sound +

Every room has its own TV, so the group can split up.

The living room is the main setup: a Samsung smart TV with a sound bar, best for a movie or the game. Streaming apps are built in, so open one and sign in.

The bedrooms have their own screens too. The master (Sony) and the 2nd bedroom use an Amazon Fire device, and the kids' room is a Samsung smart TV.

Sign into your own Netflix, Prime, Disney+, or other apps, then sign out before you leave so the next guests don't land in your accounts.

Coffee, tea & the kitchen +

There's a Nespresso machine and a Nespresso milk frother on the counter.

Pods and tea bags are right next to the coffee maker.

Sugar and stevia are in the kitchen closet; creamer pods are in the fridge.

The kitchen is stocked with cookware, dishes, and basics. Help yourself.

Laundry +

The washer and dryer are downstairs. Detergent is provided, and a normal cycle handles most loads.

BBQ on the deck +

There's a gas BBQ grill on the deck. Open the propane valve, ignite, and close the valve when you're done.

Gas only. By county rule, charcoal grills are not allowed and guests may not bring their own grills. No open fires, fire pits, or campfires anywhere on the property.

Where things are +

Extra blankets and bedding are in the walk-in closet of each of the three bedrooms.

Spares (batteries, light bulbs, a spare TV remote, the small door-release tool, and a basic toolkit with screwdrivers, a wrench, and a hammer) are in the upstairs hallway closet.

The first aid kit is in the upstairs hallway closet, next to the spares.

Fire extinguishers: one in the kitchen, one in the downstairs hallway.

Space heaters are in the downstairs bedroom closets. Use the marked outlets.

The electric breaker panel is outside, on the front wall to the left of the garage door as you face the house.

Quick fixes

Good to know

Downstairs feels cold+

Warm air rises, so the lower level runs cooler. Grab a space heater from a downstairs bedroom closet and plug it into a marked outlet. Those outlets are on their own circuit, so the heater won't trip a breaker.

Wi-Fi dropped+

The Wi-Fi is a mesh network with two units: one by the upstairs TV and one by the downstairs bedroom TV.

Start by turning Wi-Fi off and back on on your phone or laptop. That clears most hiccups.

If one part of the house is weak or offline, unplug the unit nearest you for about 30 seconds, then plug it back in and give it two minutes.

If the whole house is down, open the outdoor breaker panel and flip the breaker labeled Wi-Fi off for 30 seconds, then back on.

The network also restarts itself every night, so a stubborn problem usually clears by morning. If it's still down, message the host team on Airbnb and we'll get it back up.

Power's out in part of the house+

A breaker may have tripped. The electric panel is outside, on the front wall to the left of the garage door as you face the house. Find any switch sitting in the middle and flip it firmly off, then on.

Locked out of a bedroom or bathroom+

If a privacy lock clicks shut behind you, there's a small door-release tool in the upstairs hallway closet with the spares. Push it straight into the little hole in the center of the outside knob to pop it open.

Can't find a TV remote+

There's a spare remote in the upstairs hallway closet with the batteries and bulbs. Please leave all remotes in the house at check-out so the next guests aren't stuck.

This is bear country

Tahoe safety

🐻 Bears

Tahoe's black bears are smart, common, and after one thing: food. They're rarely aggressive, but a car or kitchen that smells of food will draw them in.

  • All trash goes in the bear bin (in the driveway, you can't miss it), latched shut. Never leave bags outside it.
  • Keep food out of the car and garage, and lock your car every time.
  • Keep ground-floor doors and windows closed when you're out or asleep.
  • See a bear? Don't run or approach. Make noise, look big, and give it room to leave.

You're at 6,200 feet

You'll feel the altitude at first. Drink more water than usual, go easy on alcohol the first day, and take it slow if you feel lightheaded or headachy. That eases as your body adjusts.

The lake is cold, respect it

Lake Tahoe stays cold year-round and there are no lifeguards. Cold water saps strength fast, so wear a life vest for paddling and keep a close eye on kids at the shore.

Winter driving

Mountain weather turns quickly. Carry chains from about November through April, keep the tank above half, and check conditions before you drive. tahoeroads.info is the best local source, or use Caltrans QuickMap or dial 511.

Fire & detectors

Gas BBQ only. No charcoal, fire pits, or campfires, and no fireworks (both illegal and dangerous here). Smoke and carbon-monoxide detectors are installed throughout, so please don't disable them.
